# Why You Might Want To Talk To Me

Hi, I am Silvan Wegmann, a lecturer at Hochschule Luzern - Informatik and part of the IoT Systems and Software research team. Students often first meet me in modules that show only one slice of what I do. This page is meant as a quick orientation: where I can help, which project ideas usually fit me well, and where it may be useful to bring a different expert into the conversation.

For questions, e-mail is usually the best starting point. I normally try to reply within 2-3 days, depending on my teaching schedule. Since I work at HSLU with an 80% part-time employment and usually keep Fridays free, replies may occasionally take a little longer around busy teaching weeks.

## Quick Fit

You are probably in the right place if your question or project touches one of these areas:

- Embedded software, close-to-hardware programming, Linux-based devices or systems programming.
- Mobile robots or robot platforms, especially when the project is about building, integrating or programming a concrete platform.
- IoT systems beyond the first prototype: updates, device management, factory-floor integration, security, compliance and operations.
- Programming languages (Rust, C++, Python in particular), compilers, WebAssembly, development tools or runtime environments.
- Practical software engineering: architecture, testing, automation, maintainability and making software robust enough for real use.
- Industry-facing student projects, especially where a working prototype or careful technical exploration matters.

I can also help you clarify whether an idea is mainly a software engineering topic, an embedded systems topic, a business-process topic, or something that needs a mixed supervision setup.

## A Useful First Message

If you write to me about a project idea, please include:

- Your study program and semester.
- The kind of work you are looking for: bachelor thesis, WIPRO, module project, quick question or industry contact.
- Two or three sentences about the idea.
- What already exists: hardware, code, company contact, data, users, constraints.
- What you would like to learn or prove.
- Any deadline or formal registration date.

## Project Shapes That Usually Work Well

You do not need a fully polished idea before contacting me. It is often enough if there is a real question, a technical risk, or a thing you want to build and learn from.

Good project shapes include:

- Building a reliable prototype around a physical device or connected machine.
- Working with a robot platform where the learning goal is in the software, integration or system behavior.
- Comparing platforms, tools or architectures with a clear technical question.
- Creating tooling, diagnostics or automated tests for embedded or IoT systems.
- Exploring whether a technology such as WebAssembly, AI tooling or a new runtime is useful in a concrete setting.
- Making a messy hardware/software integration understandable and maintainable.
- Turning an industry problem into a focused student project with a realistic scope.

## Topics I Currently Like To Coach

### Capylang and Compiler Tooling

I am working on Capylang, a compiler platform and programming-language playground. Student projects around language design, compiler construction, static analysis, tools, editors, interpreters, runtimes, WebAssembly or teaching-oriented programming environments are very welcome.

[Open the Capylang GitHub project](https://github.com/Jokymon/capylang)

### WebAssembly

I am interested in WebAssembly both as a general software technology and as a possible building block for IoT and embedded systems. This can include runtimes, sandboxing, portable code, plugin systems, tools, language integration, or the simple question of where WebAssembly is actually useful beyond web browsers.

### Close-To-Hardware Programming

I enjoy projects that bring software close to the physical world: Raspberry Pi, Raspberry Pi Pico, Arduino, ESP32, NXP microcontrollers, sensors, actuators, buses, drivers, measurement setups, hardware test rigs, device communication and robust low-level code.

### Mobile Robots and Robot Platforms

I also like working with mobile robots and robot platforms, especially when the focus is on simple platforms, programming, integration, experimentation or getting a concrete system to behave reliably. For more specialized robotics topics, I work together with people from the [HSLU AI Robotics Lab](https://www.hslu.ch/de-ch/informatik/forschung/labs/ai-robotics/) and can help you find the right contact.

### IoT, Smart Machines and Connected Products

IoT becomes interesting when it leaves the lab. I am especially interested in embedded Linux machines, software updates, security, CRA and other regulatory aspects, device fleets, production environments, factory-floor integration and the question of how teams operate connected products over time.

### Projects With Thermoplan

I previously worked as a software developer at Thermoplan and currently act as a liaison to their innovation team. This has already led to student projects around UX, VR, AI in business processes, energy-saving models for machines and other exploratory work.

Thermoplan often has its own ideas for student projects, and strong creative suggestions by students may also be worth discussing.

Thermoplan projects are usually announced through the regular HSLU student project platform. If you are interested in such a project, please first check the current entries there:

[Complesis platform](https://complesis.hslu.ch/home)

If you have your own idea for a project related to coffee machines, connected machines, or software topics around a coffee machine company, please contact me first so we can discuss whether it could fit and how to proceed.

## Modules That Match These Interests

These modules are part of my teaching work rather than open coaching topics by themselves. Still, they are useful signals for the kinds of systems, tools and questions I like to work with.

### C# In Action (CSA)

Starting in spring 2027 I will teach in a module called "C# in action" (CSA). It uses a Raspberry Pi plus NXP MK22 MCU mobile robot platform to learn systems programming in C#.

[Open the CSA module description](https://modulverzeichnis.hslu.ch/module/9008)

### Smart Machines (SMACH)

In "Smart Machines" (SMACH) I introduce students to the challenges of building an embedded-Linux-based machine software stack and integrating it into a factory-floor environment. This includes device fleets, updates, CRA, production environments and the broader reality of industrial machine software.

[Open the SMACH module description](https://modulverzeichnis.hslu.ch/module/6892)

### Development of Mechatronical Systems (MECH)

In [Development of Mechatronical Systems (MECH)](https://modulverzeichnis.hslu.ch/module/7708), I contribute to teaching industrial engineering students about software-related aspects of mechatronic systems.

### AI Robot Programming Block Week (AIRWEEK)

I also help out in [AIRWEEK](https://modulverzeichnis.hslu.ch/module/8765), a block week about programming ROS-based robot platforms. This is another good reason to contact me if you are interested in working with a mobile robot platform or want to find out which robotics contact at HSLU fits your idea.

## Where Students Often Misread My Profile

I also teach fundamentals of web technologies. That module is important, and I am happy to help you learn the basics there. But web applications are not my core research and coaching focus. If your thesis is mainly about building a standard web application, I may still help you sharpen the engineering angle, but we should check early whether another coach would fit better.

Strong overlaps with me are usually web plus something else: device integration, industrial workflows, embedded systems, compiler tooling, distributed systems, software architecture, testing, automation or a concrete technical risk.
